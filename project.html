01 / The bottleneck

CFD output has outgrown our capacity to interpret it.

Simulation capability has advanced for decades. Human analysis capacity has not kept pace.

Boundary-layer separation Vortices Mixing Shocks

These fundamental mechanisms often sit at the root of turbomachinery problems. My PhD research asks how AI and machine learning can help extract that information from CFD solutions.

Takeaway The bottleneck is no longer producing flow fields; it is understanding them.

02

02 / The research question

What is the best analysis method for AI itself?

An AI system does not have to copy the way a human expert works.

Human-style loop

Observe → hypothesize → test → repeat

Examine the solution, form a hypothesis, run a test, and iterate.

AI-native possibility

Probe the solution directly and quantitatively

Use representations and calculations that may be less intuitive to a person but more effective for an agent.

Question Instead of automating an expert's routine, can agents discover a better one?

03

03 / The project bet

Simplify the flow before asking what it means.

My 16.S893 project will explore an ambitious strand: represent a CFD solution using source and vorticity fields, then extract flow-mechanism information from that representation.

  1. Input CFD solution A detailed, high-dimensional flow field
  2. Representation Sources + vorticity A simpler physical description
  3. Interpretation Flow mechanisms The useful information we want to recover
Unknown 01

The representation is not unique.

Which arrangement and magnitude of sources and vortices makes the mechanisms most accessible?

Unknown 02

Extraction is a second problem.

Once the representation exists, how should an agent turn it into reliable mechanism information?

04

04 / The virtual research lab

Let agent teams search, test, critique, and improve.

Rather than assigning one agent the entire problem, I will explore a structured lab in which teams have distinct responsibilities and feedback travels upstream.

Team A

Represent

Generate different non-unique source-and-vorticity descriptions of the CFD solution.

→
Team B

Extract

Use each representation to identify the underlying flow mechanisms.

→
Team C

Evaluate

Hold the mechanism “answers,” assess Team B, and send feedback through the chain.

Team D

Critique

Challenge assumptions and methods throughout the research process.

Team E

Literature

Use Greitzer and Drela's textbooks to guide the other teams with domain literature.

Feedback loop

Structure Separate roles make the reasoning easier to inspect, compare, and improve.

05 / What I will learn

The goal is not one answer. It is a research process that can improve.

01

Which simplified representations make flow mechanisms easier to recover?

02

Which agent roles and feedback loops produce useful scientific progress?

03

Where do AI-native methods outperform imitation of a human workflow—and where do they fail?

Can agents discover a representation that makes the physics easier to see?

Agent capabilities are changing on a timescale of months, so the architecture must remain flexible. The constant is the scientific question—and the need to evaluate every result against physical understanding.
